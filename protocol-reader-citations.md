## NOTABLE PATTERNS

The Protocol Reader's reference graph clusters in five intellectual neighborhoods:

1. **STS and institutional sociology**: Collins, Lynch, Latour, Star, Winner, Wynne. Attention to how coordination is actually *practised*, not theorized. Focus on materiality, failure, and local knowledge.

2. **Standards history and governance**: Yates/Murphy, Russell, Shapiro/Varian, Cargill, Raymond. Long arc from telegraph to digital, emphasizing institutional struggle over protocol dominance and the economics of coordination.

3. **Protocol Institute output and Summer of Protocols essays**: Rao (foundational theory), Asparouhova (failure modes), Friend (lifecycle), Schroff (safety), Fernández (emergence). Internal coherence; direct application of protocol theory to contemporary cases.

4. **Organization theory and high-reliability systems**: Weick, Perrow, Hollnagel, Alexander, Meyer/Rowan. How organizations maintain safety and coherence under uncertainty; when procedures work vs. when they fail; the role of culture and ritual.

5. **Digital governance and networked publics**: boyd, Collins, Jenkins, Eghbal, Austin. How online communities form, sustain themselves, and coordinate through emergent protocols; friction between platform design and bottom-up practice.

**Notably absent**: mainstream AI ethics papers, alignment research, LLM safety discourse. The volume's orientation is toward institutional and coordination theory, not capability/alignment. This makes it a strong complement to technical AI work—protocol design as the missing layer between capability and institutional embedding.

**For human-AI collaboration research**: The volume frames AI coordination as a *protocol design problem*, not an alignment problem. Humans and AI systems are participants in emergent protocol systems. Effectiveness depends on clarity of rules (protocols), feedback loops (reliability engineering), role definition (Weick), and graceful degradation when assumptions fail (Perrow, Hollnagel).
