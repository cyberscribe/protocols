Where traces live

Every kind of thing we write down, placed by how reliably it actually gets loaded and how slowly it goes stale — and shown twice, because the copy you carry and the copy the AI carries are not in the same place.

The AI reads your standards more reliably than you do. You know your status better than it does.

The boundary swaps sides

For all four context types the arrow points right: crossing to the AI gains reach. For both tracking types it points left: crossing loses reach.

So the boundary does not move everything one way. It swaps which side is reliable, and it swaps on the kind of trace. That gives a rule with teeth: externalise the persistent-qualitative; keep the transient-quantitative in the loop with a person. Tracking is not context you haven’t written down yet.

The cascade is a gradient

Project → general → standards climbs up and to the right — slower decay and wider reach together. Leverage is reach divided by drift, and the axes are reach and inverse-drift, so leverage is just the product of the two.

The cascade isn’t a hierarchy drawn beside the model. It is the steepest line across it.

Templates are a different animal

They are the only sematectonic type here: a template is the work itself, unfinished, stimulating its own completion — Heylighen’s half-built wasp nest. Everything else is a marker about work.

The existing diagram already sets templates outside the cascade on intuition. The theory supplies the reason, and adds one: promoting into a template is completing work, not writing a note about it. It is not the same operation.

Status is in the pheromone corner

High reach, fast decay, quantitative — precisely a classical ant trail, and the one thing that must never be promoted upward.

Pushing a transient trace into a slow-decaying, high-reach tier is the archive trap’s mechanism, not its symptom. The cascade needs a floor, and status sits below it.

Where our taxonomy doesn’t hold up

Working standards and general reference occupy nearly the same cell. Both are marker-based, qualitative, slow-decaying, collective. They differ on reach — and reach is the loading policy, not a property of the content. That distinction is a policy wearing a content-type costume.

Projects and status share a box and differ in decay by an order of magnitude. Every other tier is decay-rate-matched. These two probably need splitting.

How to argue with it

Every position is a judgement, which is the point of plotting them: disagreement becomes concrete. Move a point and say why.

Two claims break if the positions are wrong — the context/tracking reversal, and templates as the sole sematectonic type. Both are checkable against practice rather than against opinion.

Decay rates for the four context types follow the tier drift rates in cascade.py; the two tracking rates are new and invented.
