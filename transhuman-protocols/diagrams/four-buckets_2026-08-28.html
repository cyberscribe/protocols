Four buckets

Two working memories and two durable stores. The human’s working memory decays; the AI’s is overwritten every cycle. What the person does about it is governed by one thing: whether the ritual runs on felt need, or runs regardless.

Discipline is not effort

At discipline 0 the ritual tracks felt need — you write and you go back and look when you feel you should. That is what people actually do, and it is not irrational.

But the feeling is wrong in a specific way: you notice a gap only if you have enough left to notice it with. So felt need is under-read exactly when it is highest, and it lags on top of that. Discipline is the degree to which the act stops asking the feeling for permission.

The lag alone is not enough. Delay it but keep it accurate and a confidence-driven person equilibrates to the same place as a disciplined one — discipline only bites because the signal is biased, not merely late. That is a result of the model, and an assumption of it: the bias is a dial (BLIND), not a finding.

Each side breaks the other half of the score

Poison can only ever cost you correctness; a thin head can only ever cost you fit. Neither failure shows up in the other’s number, which is why a single quality measure hides both.

And because output is a product, whichever is worse dominates. Perfect correctness on the wrong problem still scores near zero.

Poison settles, it doesn’t ratchet

Contamination reaches a fixed point, not saturation: roughly the poison rate scaled down by how well the two gates catch. At zero discernment the store converges on the poison rate itself; at the default 64% combined catch rate, a 40% poison rate settles near 6%.

An earlier version ratcheted to 100% regardless of rate. That was an artefact of the store growing rather than being replaced at capacity, and it has been corrected.

Two gates, and they multiply

Both parties inspect at promotion, so a bad item survives only if both miss it. At 45% and 35% that is a 64% catch rate — and 36% of everything bad still lands.

Neither slider alone gets you clean. That is an argument for two reviewers rather than one better one.

Two limbs, one ritual

Discipline governs both externalising (writing it down) and referencing (going back to what you wrote for context and orientation). Writing without referencing does nothing for the bucket that leaks — the store fills and the head still empties.

Still not modelled: whether discipline raises discernment, which would connect the human side to the AI side for the first time. Deliberately left open, because a positive loop produces spirals by construction.

4  promote 3 items AI WM → AI durable; a bad one is caught unless both miss: 1−(1−h)(1−a)
5  externalise human WM → human durable × ritual   — the same ritual, other limb
6  decay   human WM ×(1−d)   — nothing else in the model loses anything
   score   fit = (wh/40)0.8 · correctness = (1−poison share)1.6 · output = fit × correctness

An illustrative dynamical toy, not a fitted model: capacities, the six-items-per-session work rate, the three-per-session promotion rate and the quarter-of-durable reload are set for legibility. What it is claiming is structural — that only one bucket leaks, that only one accumulates irreversibly, and that the two review gates combine rather than substitute.
