# Protocols for Effective Human-AI Collaboration

Human-AI collaboration is the externalisation of human intentions into a non-human system that can act on it as either supplement to, or replacement of, human activity. These collaborations also enable results impossible to achieve by humans alone — a new category of transhuman work. Protocols are the mechanism by which transhuman work is initiated, refined, coordinated, monitored, validated, and completed.

Effective human collaboration methodologies share design properties — data gathering, clarification practices, inspectability and monitoring, accountability through roles and trust calibration, treatment of shared context at multiple horizons, and standards for language, tooling, and behaviour. These recur across traditions over time, including modern paradigms designed for knowledge work (GTD, agile, pair programming). Where human methodologies manifest as habits, rituals, and ceremonies, transhuman collaborations require protocols.

The hypothesis: human collaboration paradigms can be transposed into transhuman protocols by deriving fundamental characteristics from concrete implementations of the human paradigms, and recasting them for transhuman use.

The thread also pursues a definition of effectiveness richer than output-vs-intent — one that accounts for cognitive load, sovereignty, and debt at the individual level, and governance, bias, and social impact at the group level. The aim is a holistic measure for applying transhuman practice within existing systems of human collaboration.
