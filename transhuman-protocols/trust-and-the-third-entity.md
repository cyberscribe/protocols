# Trust and the third entity

*Draft theory note — 2026-07-08. Feeds the effectiveness-definition strand of the research statement
(cognitive load / sovereignty / debt) and the protocol-description work. Empirical instrument for the
constructs below: the PeakePro cognition assessment (`~/workspace/projects/peakepro-diagnostics/
cognition-assessment/`, esp. `dynamics-model.md`).*

---

## The claim

In transhuman collaboration, **trust is not a property of the AI.** It is a moving locus, and its
mature form attaches to a thing that did not exist before the collaboration began: the **relationship**
— the human-with-AI-and-protocol working dyad, a third entity distinct from either party. The protocol
is what builds that entity, and the surplus that defines transhuman work — results impossible for the
human alone — lives in the relationship, not in either agent.

This reframes the effectiveness question the research statement poses. "Trust calibration" is named
there as an accountability property of effective collaboration. The refinement: what is being
calibrated is not confidence in a model's outputs but confidence in a *system one has built* — and,
through it, in a *relationship one is party to*.

## Three loci

Trust migrates through three positions as a collaboration matures:

1. **The model.** Naive trust: you believe the output because it is fluent and usually right. Borrowed
   confidence — you can neither inspect nor justify it. This is where most users begin and many stay.
2. **The system (Π).** Earned trust: you have built scaffolding around the model — curated context,
   explicit intent, verification tooling, decision principles — and you trust *that*. Π is portable,
   inspectable, and yours. You can rationally distrust the raw model and still hold high, justified
   confidence in the system, because the system is engineered to catch what the model gets wrong.
3. **The relationship.** The endpoint: you trust the working partnership itself — a third entity with
   its own track record, habits, and division of labour. System-trust is what *licenses* it. You do
   not trust the model; you trust what the two of you, plus the protocol, reliably produce together.

The mistake the naive locus makes is not "too much trust" but **trust in the wrong object.** The cure
is not less trust; it is relocating trust onto something that can bear it.

## Π is the protocol, and learning is how it is built

Π — the system one trusts — is exactly what this research programme calls a *protocol*: the codified
mechanism for initiating, refining, monitoring, and validating transhuman work. The note the dynamics
model adds is the **mechanism by which Π accretes**: the learning function.

Each interaction is an **experiment.** The human states an intention; the AI fulfils it to some degree
of *(accuracy, suitability)*; and where the outcome falls short, the human does root-cause analysis on
the controllable inputs —

- **context** (what the system knew),
- **intent expression** (how the goal and "done" were framed),
- **tool availability** (verification and grounding: tests, example inspection, retrieval, MCP),
- **assumptions** (the priors shaping the first two — usually the root cause),

— and deposits the lesson as a durable practice. That deposit *is* a line of protocol. Run the loop
enough times and the accumulated deposits are Π: a working library of what reliably turns intention
into outcome with this partner, on this kind of work.

This is why the research statement's central conjecture holds mechanically. Human collaboration
paradigms (GTD, agile, pair programming) are *frozen learning* — the systematised residue of countless
experiments in how humans coordinate. Transposing them into transhuman protocols is the same operation
run deliberately: extract the invariant practice, recast it for a non-human collaborator. The learning
loop is protocol-formation observed live; a human methodology is protocol-formation already complete.

**Learning is the transmission, not a force among forces.** A verification you perform once and never
systematise is a one-off; the trust it earns fades and the state reverts. Only when the lesson enters Π
does an in-the-moment success become durable capability. No learning function, no protocol, no third
entity — just repeated, forgettable transactions with a model.

## Why the relationship is mechanism, not metaphor

Calling the dyad a "relationship" would be sentimental if the medium did not force it. It does. The
channel of transhuman work is **natural language** — an inherently *social* medium. Exchanging in it
activates anthropomorphism *perforce*: the human cannot help but model the counterpart as an agent
with intent (the media-equation / "computers are social actors" finding — Reeves & Nass 1996; the
anthropomorphism account — Epley, Waytz & Cacioppo 2007). The relationship framing is not a choice of
tone; it is what the interface does to the human whether or not they consent.

This cuts both ways, and the two edges are the same edge:

- It is **what makes a relationship possible.** A third entity can form because the human already
  treats the exchange as social. The scaffolding of trust has something to attach to.
- It is **what makes trust systematically miscalibrated.** Because the counterpart reads as social, a
  fluent, confident error lands as a *betrayal* rather than a tool fault — and over-weights past the
  base rate it warrants. Conversely, sustained fluency reads as *reliability* and invites over-trust.
  The same social read that enables the relationship guarantees the trust in it starts wrong.

So miscalibration is not a user failing to be corrected once; it is a standing property of the medium.
Protocols earn their keep precisely here: the practices that re-anchor trust to the base rate, that
keep a record of where the partner actually fails, that separate the social read from the empirical
one — these are not niceties, they are the counterweight to a bias the channel installs by default.

## Sovereignty is the qualifier

Two collaborations can show identical, high reliance and be opposite in kind. What separates them is
**cognitive sovereignty** — whether the human holds the direction and the final decision, and whether
they *built* the system they are trusting.

- High reliance, low sovereignty → trust in the **model** (ceded — you believe the output because you
  did not build anything to check it). Unjustified, and the seedbed of cognitive debt: capability
  quietly transfers out.
- High reliance, high sovereignty → trust in the **relationship** (held — you built Π, you run the
  loop, you own the call). Justified, and generative: the same reliance that would deskill a passive
  user upskills the one who is party to the partnership rather than a passenger in it.

Sovereignty is therefore the condition under which trust may safely move up the loci. Without it, trust
migrates to the model and stops there. With it, trust can rest on the relationship because the human
remains one of its authors.

## Consequences for the library

1. **An effectiveness marker.** The research statement wants effectiveness measured against evolving
   intent *and* wider contextual implications (load / sovereignty / debt). *Justified,
   inspectable relationship-trust* is a candidate marker: not "does the human trust the AI" (which
   rewards the naive locus) but "is the human's confidence attached to a system they can inspect and a
   relationship they co-author." A protocol that raises reliance while trust stays lodged in the model
   is scoring the wrong thing.

2. **Protocols as trust-relocation devices.** Reading the library through this lens: a good protocol is
   one that moves trust off the model and onto Π — by making context explicit, verification cheap,
   assumptions surfaced, and the failure record legible. That is a describable design property, gradable
   across protocols.

3. **Generalisation to other minds.** The research statement flags inter-neurotype and inter-species
   protocols as productive contrasts. The third-entity frame travels: any protocol between different
   *kinds* of mind builds a third thing that is neither party, and trust in it is likewise earned
   through systematised learning rather than assumed from fluency. The natural-language case is the one
   where the social read is *forced*; other channels may make it optional, which is itself a distinguishing
   property worth mapping.

## Open

- **Naming.** "Relationship" imports warmth that may overclaim. Alternatives: *the dyad*, *the working
  system*, *the compound agent*. The claim is structural regardless of the word; the word is a
  register choice.
- **Measuring the locus.** Can an instrument distinguish model-trust from relationship-trust
  behaviourally (not just by self-report)? The sovereignty × reliance cross is the current proxy; a
  dedicated learning-function measure is the candidate sharpening.
- **The erosion nonlinearity.** If anthropomorphism-driven over-weighting is structural, it should be
  measurable and should scale with felt human-likeness of the exchange — a testable prediction and a
  hook for empirical protocol evaluation.
