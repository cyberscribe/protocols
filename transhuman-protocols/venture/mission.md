## Mission statement

**You don't have to trust AI. You can trust your practice.**

There are systems, rituals, principles, and best practices you can put in play so that trust never
has to rest on the model itself. We research, teach, and install methodologies of practice in
relation to AI that let individuals and teams consistently deliver good results in appropriate
timing — while growing, not eroding, their own capability.

The mechanism is the research thread's central finding (`../trust-and-the-third-entity.md`): mature
trust in human-AI work attaches not to the model but to the system of practice you build around it —
inspectable, portable, and yours — and through it to the working relationship that system makes
possible. A good protocol is a trust-relocation device. The business teaches people to build them.

Three commitments follow:

1. **Method over model.** Reliability comes from protocol — context, intent, verification,
   surfaced assumptions — not from the model's fluency. Clients leave with a methodology that
   survives model churn.
2. **Sovereignty over dependency.** The human holds direction and final decision, and builds the
   system they trust. Same reliance, opposite outcome: upskilling instead of cognitive debt.
3. **Growth over shortcut.** AI as gym buddy and spotter, not answer machine
   (`../knowing-was-never-the-point.md`). The practice is designed so the human gets stronger by
   using it.

## Why the name must change

Pope Leo XIV's encyclical *Magnifica Humanitas* has given "transhumanism" a decisively negative
public valence — sci-fi singularity, AGI replacement, the person reduced to output. That register
now actively misrepresents us.

The irony worth preserving: **our position is aligned with the encyclical, not opposed to it.**
Human dignity not depending on output, struggle as the site of growth, the person as capacity
rather than product — these are load-bearing claims in our own corpus. The current name puts us on
the wrong side of a debate we are actually winning arguments *for*. The new name should signal
partnership with human growth, not the machinery that threatens it.

## Naming criteria

- **Positive valence** — growth, craft, practice, partnership. Nothing that reads as
  human-replacement or human-modification.
- **Points at the method, not the machine.** The differentiator is the methodology of practice;
  "AI" need not appear at all.
- **Carries both surfaces** — research library credibility and consulting/training brand.
- **Survives its audiences** — enterprise buyers, protocol researchers, general public, and
  (given the encyclical's reach) religiously-informed skeptics.
- **Avoids the generic** — not another "AI enablement" consultancy-speak construction.

## Seed directions (divergent — nothing decided)

Families to draw from, not shortlisted names:

- **Practice/craft** — apprenticeship, discipline, craft register. Emphasises the human doing the
  work well.
- **Protocol/method** — keeps continuity with the research identity; risk of reading cold or
  technical.
- **Strength/gymnasium** — the essay's muscle-and-reps frame; growth through structured effort.
- **Third entity/partnership** — names the working relationship itself; the most distinctive claim
  we own, but hardest to make legible on a business card.
