# The DRIVE method

*2026-07-08. The named methodology for effective human–AI interaction — the practical surface of the
research thread (`../README.md`) and the premise (`premise.md`). This is the asset that outlives the
tooling: a teachable, transferable discipline, in the tradition of GTD.*

---

## Why a new method (the GTD patch)

GTD optimised the era when **execution was the bottleneck**: capture every next-action, get it out of
your head, grind the list down. The whole system assumes *doing* is what's scarce — so it barely prices
*what deserves doing* (prioritisation is GTD's afterthought) and doesn't model *whether the work came
out right* (verification isn't in it at all).

When execution collapses in cost, the bottleneck moves to the two ends GTD is weakest at: **judgment at
the front** (what's worth doing, to what standard) and **verification at the back** (is what came back
actually right, and what do I learn from the gap). DRIVE inverts GTD's centre of gravity — from
throughput of execution to discernment and validation, with production delegated in the middle.

The spine stays constant: **you don't have to trust the AI — you trust your practice in relation to it.**
Or, in the method's own metaphor: *you trust your driving, not the vehicle.* The vehicle (model) changes,
gets faster, gets swapped; the driving (practice) is the durable, transferable skill.

## The loop

**D**ecide · **R**equest · **I**nspect · **V**alidate · **E**volve

| Step | What it is | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|
| **D — Decide** | Choose what's worth doing and to what standard; define "done" and why it matters | The scarce skill under cheap execution; GTD's blind spot. Prioritisation is step one, not an afterthought |
| **R — Request** | Express intent, context, and done-criteria — and surface your own assumptions | Sets the partner up to succeed. Assumptions live here because they are the usual root cause of a bad result (`../trust-and-the-third-entity.md`) |
| **I — Inspect** | Read it for the initial "tells" of hallucination — surface signals of a bad result before you believe it | Breaks the anthropomorphic reflex to accept fluent output on trust; a cheap first-pass filter before the costlier Validate |
| **V — Validate** | Confirm it against your done-criteria, tests, or ground truth | Re-anchors trust to the base rate, not the fluency — the "relationship counsellor" move. Distinct from Inspect: Inspect reads for *tells*, Validate *confirms it holds up* |
| **E — Evolve** | Root-cause any gap, then update **two things: your context and your practice** | Updating context feeds straight back into the next **R**equest (closing the loop); updating practice is what accretes into the system you trust instead of the model (Π). No update, no learning, no method — just repeated transactions |

Delegation sits implicitly between **Request** and **Inspect** — the cheap middle, handed to the AI.

## Practitioners: DRIVErs

People who run the method are **DRIVErs**. It's playful but load-bearing: a driver is *in control and
directing* — the sovereignty claim in one word. You are not a passenger in the work; you are driving it,
and you trust your driving.

## Naming status — precedent check (2026-07-08)

"DRIVE" is **usable but a crowded shell**. The exact niche (a personal practice for human–AI *interaction*)
and the Decide-Request-Inspect-Validate-Evolve expansion returned **no precedent** — that part is novel.
But the name is not cleanly ownable:

- **Roman Rackwitz's "DRIVE Method"** — an existing marketed method in knowledge-work productivity
  (behavioral design / intrinsic motivation). The near-exact hit.
- **"Students' DRIVE"** (ScienceDirect, 2025) — a framework to evaluate learning through interactions
  with generative AI. Same conceptual space.
- **Dan Pink's *Drive*** — not a method, but the loud prior association for "Drive" in knowledge work.
- **"DRIVE Framework" for AI ROI/prioritisation** — another DRIVE+AI entrant, different use.

**Decision — committed to DRIVE (2026-07-08).** It runs as the house methodology; differentiation comes
from always using it as a lockup ("the DRIVE method" / "the DRIVE loop") and leading with the novel
five-step expansion, which is unclaimed. Coexistence with Rackwitz's DRIVE Method and Pink's *Drive* is
accepted. If trademark-grade protection is later wanted, the **expansion** — not the bare word — is the
defensible mark.
