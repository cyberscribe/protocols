# Protocols for Co-Cognition — symposium talk notes

**Audience:** Protocol Institute symposium; Formal Protocol Theory and Protocols for Business communities  
**Length:** 25 minutes, plus discussion  
**Core proposition:** AI adoption becomes durable when the tacit interaction regime is made legible, protocolized, and evaluated across both immediate performance and future capability.  
**Evidence language:** `[theory]`, `[internal-exploratory]`, and `[external-causal]` identify the standing of the material without interrupting the argument.

## Slide 1 — Protocols for Co-Cognition

**Timing: 0:00–1:00**

I want to put a specific object in front of both halves of this Institute: the recurring arrangement by which a person, an AI system, and an organisation divide cognition and authority.

That arrangement already has rules. It determines who frames the goal, who generates, who judges, who checks, who repairs, and what knowledge survives. Most of the time those rules are tacit. They are inherited from an interface, a manager, a deadline, or yesterday’s habit.

My proposition is that this tacit arrangement is a protocol problem. For protocol theory, it offers a compact new setting in which rules, states, asymmetries, composition, and evolution can be studied. For business, it offers an operating layer between “we gave people AI” and “we created durable value.”

The assessment I have been developing is useful here as an observability layer. It helps us estimate the current human position in the arrangement. The postures are guideposts. The protocols are the interventions. The business outcomes are performance now, reconstructability later, and the distribution of responsibility across the workflow.

## Slide 2 — Every business already runs an AI protocol

**Timing: 1:00–2:30**

Here is the first business claim: every organisation using generative AI already runs an AI protocol, whether it has named one or not.

Look at the six allocations. Goal formation: who defines success and acceptance criteria? Generation: who produces the candidate? Judgement: who decides it is adequate, and on what basis? Verification: who gathers evidence? Repair: who owns disagreement and error? Retention: what rationale, skill, and context remain available next time?

If those decisions are not explicit, the protocol does not disappear. It defaults to the incentives around the work. A speed target can quietly make fluency the acceptance criterion. A blanket “use AI first” rule can give a worker responsibility without meaningful authorship. A recipient can become the de facto quality-control function for work they did not commission and cannot easily reconstruct.

This is why the object is larger than a prompt. A prompt governs one response. A protocol governs the work relationship: entry conditions, roles, evidence, handoffs, exceptions, recourse, and memory.

For a business audience, this is good news. It means the organisation can improve AI work without waiting for a settled model landscape. The durable levers are decision rights, evidence, repair, and retention. Those survive model and interface churn.

## Slide 3 — Adoption is access. Protocolization is redesign.

**Timing: 2:30–4:00**

Adoption dashboards tell us whether capability is present: licences, active users, tokens, tasks, hours saved, outputs shipped. Those measures are operationally useful. They describe dose and throughput.

Protocolization asks how the work system changed around the capability. Who now owns the decision? Where did verification move? Who absorbs rework? Can someone explain the result when the model is unavailable or has changed? Did expertise broaden, concentrate, or decay? Can the organisation recover from a plausible but fluent error?

This is the same complementarity problem seen with earlier general-purpose technologies. Substitution produces some value; redesign produces the larger and more durable value. In AI, protocols are a plausible class of complementary organisational capital because they encode a new division of cognitive labour.

The commercial implication is concrete. A workflow can post better local productivity while building liabilities elsewhere: recipient rework, slower verification, fragmented accountability, weak provenance, or dependence on a capability that cannot be reconstructed. Conversely, a small amount of well-placed ceremony can make delegation safer and faster by clarifying what “done” means and when checking can stop.

So the managerial question shifts from “How much AI are we using?” to “What interaction regime are we standardising, and what does it optimise across time and across the chain?”

## Slide 4 — A unit, a state, and an operator

**Timing: 4:00–6:00**

The theoretical contribution can be stated in three parts.

First, the unit of analysis is the **interaction regime**: the recurrent distribution of cognitive operations and decision rights across person, model, and organisation. This is richer than “AI use” because it preserves the structure that plausibly produces outcomes.

Second, **posture** is a provisional state estimate of that regime as experienced and enacted by the human participant. Trust captures expected usefulness and the disposition to rely. Sovereignty captures retained authorship of goals, criteria, and consequential judgement. Load and Debt describe costs around the arrangement. A state estimate is valuable precisely because it can be updated when task, stakes, evidence, system, or policy changes.

Third, a **protocol** is a conditional operator on that state and regime. In the notation on the slide, the next co-cognitive state depends on the current state, the protocol, context, and the episode’s outcome and feedback.

This produces two distinct research questions. Protocol fit asks which procedure performs best from a given state in a given context. Protocol transition asks how repeated use of a procedure changes the state itself. The second question matters enormously for firms: today’s workflow may be training tomorrow’s workforce, for better or worse.

This formulation also gives formal protocol theory something tractable to work with: roles, messages, admissible transitions, traces, failure states, refinement, and composition—with legitimacy and accountability attached to the human and institutional side of an asymmetric system.

## Slide 5 — The procedure changes the outcome

**Timing: 6:00–8:00**

The strongest reason to treat protocol as a causal object comes from experiments that hold much of the technology constant while changing the interaction arrangement.

In Bastani and colleagues’ field experiment with nearly a thousand students, a general GPT-4 interface increased assisted practice performance by 48 percent relative to control, while that group later scored 17 percent below control on an unaided exam. A tutor-scaffolded interface using the same underlying model and teacher-supplied solutions produced a 127 percent assisted improvement, while later unaided performance was statistically indistinguishable from control.

That is a profound business result in miniature. Immediate assisted performance and future independent capability are separate outcome horizons. Interaction rules change how much reasoning remains human work. The same model can sit inside different capability trajectories.

Dell’Acqua and colleagues show the complementary task-side result. With 758 consultants, GPT-4 improved performance inside a jagged capability frontier and reduced correctness on a task outside it. The operational rule therefore has to be conditional on task and demonstrated capability. “Use the tool” is too coarse; “delegate under these conditions, test these edges, and escalate here” is a protocol.

This is the promise behind the assessment and postures: identify the current regime, select the smallest useful protocol, and measure both the coupled system now and the human or organisation that remains later.

## Slide 6 — The assessment makes the hidden regime observable

**Timing: 8:00–9:30**

The assessment is a survey-based state estimator under active development. Its frozen 13 August form contains 33 unique items. It places the respondent on continuous Trust and Sovereignty axes; scores extraneous Load, Sovereignty, and perceived Cognitive Debt; and carries reported verification as an auxiliary index. Two germane-effort items are retained for research rather than combined with Load.

That split matters conceptually. Deliberate comprehension and framing can be productive investment. Rework friction, diffuse supervision, and residual vigilance are different costs. A protocol may increase the first while reducing the second. A single undifferentiated workload score would conceal that mechanism.

Dissonance has been retired from scoring. Its history produced a useful construct-development result and a cleaner architecture: the current empirical model makes no claim from a DISS score. Lower-case dissonance remains available as a future, independently specified process hypothesis around expectation violation and repair.

The instrument’s immediate business value is diagnostic and generative. It gives teams a disciplined vocabulary for asking where judgement sits, where effort is going, and what capability may be left unfollowed. Its scientific value is as a router candidate: do these state variables predict which protocol produces better independent outcomes?

That is the bridge from assessment to protocol theory. Observability precedes controlled transition.

## Slide 7 — Postures are guideposts in a continuous state space

**Timing: 9:30–11:00**

The map uses Trust horizontally and retained Sovereignty vertically. The four names are memorable regions, while the continuous coordinates do the analytical work.

Partnership combines high reliance with active, reason-responsive human judgement. Hypervigilance combines retained judgement with checking that may exceed the value of additional evidence. Overreliance combines high reliance with too little active judgement for the task or future capability requirement. Compliance combines weak trust and weak authorship, often under external constraint.

These are guideposts rather than identities. The same person can be in Partnership while drafting in a familiar domain, Hypervigilance while verifying unfamiliar analysis, Overreliance under time pressure, and Compliance inside a mandated workflow. That mobility is theoretically productive: a position that moves with context can be influenced by protocol and policy.

For business, the four regions act as a routing interface. They distinguish four problems that are often collapsed into “training”: extend good collaboration, convert verification into assurance, preserve judgement and capability, or repair institutional decision rights and recourse. Each calls for a different intervention.

The map is therefore best read as a hypothesis about fit. Its value will be established prospectively when continuous baseline state improves outcome prediction and moderates a randomized protocol effect.

## Slide 8 — Four signals point from dose toward structure

**Timing: 11:00–13:00**

The internal evidence is a discovery map. Four estimates shape the next studies.

Trust and Sovereignty were strongly but incompletely opposed: a pooled correlation of minus point five zero. That is the structural opening for a two-dimensional map. Reliance and retained judgement often trade off, but they do not collapse into one axis.

A coarse self-reported usage rung carried almost no linear information about the historical Load score in this restricted-range sample: minus point zero zero three. The interval is wide enough to keep moderate relationships open, and the historical Load items varied partly by usage rung. The useful proposition is that dose is a weak baseline and that task mode, decision rights, and protocol deserve a direct out-of-sample comparison.

The technical versus non-technical split was similarly uninformative for historical Load. That moves the business question closer to task topology: checkability, reversibility, stakes, expertise, downstream impact, voluntariness, and who owns the decision.

Finally, in one sample of 61, Sovereignty retained a partial association with lower reported Debt after Trust was held constant: minus point two nine. This is a directional signal toward the location of judgement as a capability-maintenance mechanism.

Together these estimates suggest a research programme: measure the structure of use, randomize authorship and verification, and validate against behaviour rather than using exposure or job title as the theory.

## Slide 9 — Different postures imply different business protocols

**Timing: 13:00–15:00**

If the map is useful, it should change what we do.

For Partnership, the opportunity is extension without losing calibration as capability moves. The protocol can be light: human-stated criteria, bounded delegation, sampled checks on consequential outputs, assumptions, and a brief decision record.

For Hypervigilance, the problem is not simply effort. It is verification without allocation. A stakes classification, critical-edge checks, independent tests, evidence thresholds, and a stopping rule can convert ambient scepticism into targeted assurance. The business metric is error detection per unit of checking and the residual uncertainty after the task.

For Overreliance, the objective is to preserve benefit while restoring active judgement and reconstructability. Ask for a human prediction or criteria before output, an explanation in the worker’s own words, plausible-error challenges, and occasional delayed unaided transfer. The metric is what can be explained, detected, or reproduced later.

For Compliance, an individual prompt is the wrong scale. The protocol belongs in organisational design: a named accountable owner, an option to decline or escalate, acceptable-use boundaries, sender-side quality gates, and measurement of recipient rework. The metric is whether responsibility, authority, and repair cost line up.

This is selective friction: minimum ceremony where collaboration is healthy; productive friction where learning, assurance, or accountability needs reinforcement.

## Slide 10 — Organisational maturity and individual posture must compose

**Timing: 15:00–16:30**

The Protocol Institute’s organisational work and this individual state model describe two layers of the same system.

Durable AI Adoption distinguishes governed and cultivated tracks: formal policy, procurement, guardrails, exploration, and practitioner-developed workflows need to mature together. Posture asks what settlement an individual actually inhabits inside that environment. The two can diverge.

A mature policy environment can coexist with low personal fit: people comply with a well-documented process while judgement atrophies or repair costs move downstream. Strong personal practice can coexist with weak organisational maturity: local experts make AI work safely, but the system depends on tacit heroes and fails to scale. Durable adoption appears when organisational protocol and person-level interaction regime reinforce one another.

The scale correspondence offers researchable links. Organisational disorientation—production outrunning shared understanding—may appear one person down as Cognitive Debt. Tempo mismatch—output exceeding verification capacity—may appear as transferred or residual Load. Extended blast radius—consequences spreading beyond the point of delegation—may involve ceded Sovereignty and unclear decision ownership.

This gives business a paired diagnostic: measure the surrounding protocol and the lived posture. The gap between them is itself an operational risk signal.

## Slide 11 — Protocol design is selective friction

**Timing: 16:30–18:30**

The six allocations become a practical design surface.

Criteria before output place friction at goal formation and judgement. They make delegation easier because adequacy has a boundary. Risk-tiered checks and a stopping rule place friction at verification. They prevent both blind acceptance and endless checking. An own-words explanation and delayed unaided transfer place friction at retention. They turn “I saw a good answer” into “I can reconstruct the decision.” A named owner, sender-side quality gate, and escalation path place friction at a consequential organisational handoff.

The design principle is not maximal human effort. It is efficient allocation of attention. Add human work where it produces learning, assurance, or accountability; remove it where the system has demonstrated reliable capability and the consequence is bounded.

This is also where a protocol registry becomes valuable. Each primitive can carry preconditions, target failure mode, required evidence, expected transition, maturity grade, and known externalities. Protocols can be composed into task-specific families and refined as evidence accumulates. A registry makes organisational learning portable while preserving provenance.

DRIVE fits here as a harvested bundle of recurring primitives—define, retain judgement, inspect, verify, explain or externalise—not as the centre of the theory. The theory is the allocation problem; the registry is the infrastructure; specific kits are testable compositions.

## Slide 12 — Does protocol literacy transfer inward?

**Timing: 18:30–20:00**

This symposium gives us an unusually relevant cohort. Before the results arrive, I want the interpretive branches to be visible.

The question is whether people immersed in protocol thinking organise their own human–AI relationship differently from prior respondents on the continuous axes and costs.

If the community shows higher retained Sovereignty, that suggests protocol literacy may transfer into personal practice. The next step would be to identify the mediating practices rather than celebrate a label.

If the community is indistinguishable, the result is equally useful for the programme: abstract expertise does not automatically rewrite one’s own tacit protocol. That strengthens the business case for embedded workflow rules, observability, and deliberate practice rather than awareness alone.

If the cohort is too small for a stable estimate, we retain the direction, state the uncertainty, and treat recruitment as part of the research infrastructure.

The business implication across all three branches is straightforward. Protocol competence can be treated as a capability to cultivate, while consequential workflows still need explicit rules, evidence, and recourse. Culture and governance are complements.

## Slide 13 — The first decisive experiment treats protocol as the intervention

**Timing: 20:00–22:30**

The lowest-hanging causal study holds AI access, model, interface, and task family constant and randomizes the protocol.

In a within-person crossover, each participant completes comparable tasks under three conditions. Open interaction approximates the default regime. A sovereignty-retaining condition requires success criteria and an initial plan or judgement before model output, with the final decision justified against those criteria. A risk-tiered verification condition requires classification of consequential claims, independent checks on critical edges, and a stopping rule.

The outcomes cover four horizons. Immediate performance includes blinded quality, correctness, time, and critical-error detection. Process includes where production, coordination, verification, and repair workload occur, plus acceptance, revision, override, and checking logs. Delayed capability includes unaided reproduction, transfer, explanation, plausible-error detection, and recovery when the model is unavailable or changed. Repeated state measures ask whether Trust, Sovereignty, extraneous Load, and Debt move after exposure.

Baseline continuous posture enters as a moderator. A protocol-by-state interaction would establish the assessment as a router. Within-person state change following repeated exposure would establish protocols as transition operators. Phase-specific workload would distinguish productive friction from wasted supervision.

For participating firms, this is a practical trial design rather than a distant laboratory ideal: real task families, controlled protocol variants, operational metrics, and a delayed resilience check.

## Slide 14 — One programme, two sets of deliverables

**Timing: 22:30–24:00**

For protocol theory, this programme offers four connected problems.

Structure: represent roles, messages, states, transitions, traces, composition, and refinement across human, model, and organisation. Semantics and legitimacy: specify who has standing to set criteria, whose judgement is authoritative, where responsibility sits, and what recourse makes the protocol acceptable. Dynamics and evolution: study how protocols update as models change and how repeated use transforms human and institutional state. Valuation: account for immediate output, verification cost, retained capability, externalities, and resilience across time and across recipients.

For business, the same programme produces concrete infrastructure. A maturity-by-posture diagnostic exposes gaps between policy and lived work. A versioned protocol registry turns local practice into reusable organisational knowledge. Consequential-handoff gates carry criteria, evidence, provenance, owner, and escalation. An outcome ledger records cycle time and quality alongside rework, error propagation, traceability, expertise distribution, and recovery.

The shared layer is evidence governance. Claims registers, preregistrations, protocol versions, change logs, and outcome traces are themselves meta-protocols. They let a fast-moving practice learn without quietly rewriting its history.

That combination—formal representation, legitimate decision rights, adaptive evolution, and multi-horizon value—is where the Institute can make this project much stronger than either a survey or a workflow kit on its own.

## Slide 15 — The next layer of AI adoption is protocolization

**Timing: 24:00–25:00**

The closing proposition is simple.

AI use becomes governable when we make the interaction regime legible: who frames, generates, judges, verifies, repairs, and retains.

It becomes adaptable when we treat posture as a state estimate rather than a fixed type: a guide to where the current coordination tension lies.

It becomes scientifically and commercially useful when protocols are explicit interventions whose effects can be measured across immediate performance, the distribution of work, delayed capability, and state transition.

The invitation to the Protocol Institute is threefold. Help formalise the protocol primitives and their composition. Help firms run joint organisational-maturity and individual-posture field studies. Help build a versioned evidence and protocol registry that can distinguish promising practice from earned standard.

The business opportunity is not another layer of prompting advice. It is an operating system for durable human–AI coordination—one that makes judgement, evidence, accountability, and learning visible enough to design.

**Close:** Every human–AI relationship already runs on a protocol. The practical question is whether we can read it, improve it, and learn from the transition.

## Slide 16 — Appendix: terms, precisely

Use this slide for definitional questions. Emphasise the scale distinction: the interaction regime is the full arrangement; posture is its provisional human-side state estimate; protocol is the conditional rule-set that reorganises it. Describe human–AI grounding as operational common ground without symmetrical commitment. The interaction is bidirectional within an episode, while accountability, stakes, persistence, and control over updates remain asymmetrically distributed.

## Slide 17 — Appendix: evidence register

The external record establishes coordination as a promising intervention level: average augmentation with heterogeneous synergy; task-frontier effects; large gains on bounded writing; interaction rules that changed later unaided learning; and divergence between perceived and measured speed in a specific developer study.

The internal record supplies discovery directions and measurement architecture. Keep the evidence types separate: external causal results motivate the level of intervention; internal associations motivate the variables, mechanisms, and next designs.
