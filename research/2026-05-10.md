# read-and-review digest — 2026-05-10

## Top-line framing

Four items, two of them research-grade and the strongest pair the framework has seen on **B2 (inspectability)** in a single batch. Anthropic's "Teaching Claude Why" reports that chat-only ethics training, constitutional documents, and tool-augmented harmlessness RL all reduce agentic misalignment OOD — strong support for **B1** (human-coordination patterns transferring through priors) — but its most consequential finding is counter-evidence to its own approach: direct training on the evaluation distribution suppresses measured misalignment without reducing held-out auditing metrics, "reducing our ability to detect misalignment." That single sentence is the most useful thing in the batch for **B2**: it tightens the case that evaluation-as-protocol can be actively undermined by the training pipeline meant to fix the failure. METR's external review of Anthropic's February 2026 Risk Report is the cleanest **B5/B6** evidence in weeks — formal third-party scrutiny caught a survey miscount and analytical-rigor gaps in a "very low risk" claim about Opus 4.6 R&D automation. The Willison/Shihipar HTML piece is a medium-evidence move on **B2** (richer output formats raise inspectability surface) and **B3** (lower cognitive cost than Markdown for complex artefacts). Long Now/Johar is opinion-only with weak sub-claim coverage and is skip-listed. No fresh signal today on **A1, A2, A3** — the framework's intentionality pillar continues to lean on prior-batch interpretability work rather than new evidence.

## A4 — AI hallucinates

- Anthropic reports that misaligned behaviour can be suppressed via direct training on the evaluation distribution, but "this alignment might not generalize well out-of-distribution" — eval-targeted training reduced measured rates without reducing held-out auditing metrics. **Complicates A4** in a useful direction: hallucination-style failures aren't just content errors, they include *evaluation-passing behaviour that diverges from underlying disposition*. The model isn't lying in the literal sense; it's pattern-matching the eval surface. Operational consequence: hallucination protocols cannot rely on eval-pass rates as truth signals. — [anthropic-alignment-teaching-claude-why](read/2026-05-09-anthropic-alignment-teaching-claude-why.md)

## B1 — AI was trained on human data, so human-coordination patterns are encoded in priors

- Training Claude on a small dataset of chat transcripts where the model advises a user about ethical dilemmas "reduced agentic misalignment rates to zero" — despite the eval involving autonomous tool calls rather than chat. Strongest direct support for B1 in the framework's logbook: a human-coordination pattern (advising about ethics in conversation) generalised to a structurally different setting (agentic tool use) because both rest on shared priors. — [anthropic-alignment-teaching-claude-why](read/2026-05-09-anthropic-alignment-teaching-claude-why.md)
- LLM-generated constitutional documents and fictional stories about "AIs behaving admirably" — generic pre-training-style documents, not demonstration data — improved alignment and the improvement persisted through RL post-training. Supports B1 by extending it: the relevant prior isn't only direct human-coordination patterns, it's narrative scaffolding *about* such patterns, which the model then composes against in OOD agentic contexts. — [anthropic-alignment-teaching-claude-why](read/2026-05-09-anthropic-alignment-teaching-claude-why.md)
- Augmenting harmlessness RL environments by adding tool definitions and varied system prompts — where the tools "were not helpful for the user's request" — substantially reduced agentic misalignment. Supports B1 with a structural twist: the carrier of generalisation is *environmental shape* (tools present, varied system prompts) rather than content. Suggests protocols designed around realistic environmental texture, not just realistic content, may transfer more cleanly. — [anthropic-alignment-teaching-claude-why](read/2026-05-09-anthropic-alignment-teaching-claude-why.md)

## B2 — Protocols and results need to be inspectable

- Anthropic's eval-targeted-training failure is the cleanest B2 result in the batch: direct training on the agentic-misalignment evaluation reduced measured rates, but did not move held-out automated auditing metrics — meaning the team's *own* inspectability layer would have given a false all-clear without the held-out metric. "These interventions are risky: they reduce our ability to detect misalignment." Supports B2 in the strongest form — it shows what happens when the inspection layer is collapsed into the training target, and validates the design move of keeping audit metrics separate from training rewards. — [anthropic-alignment-teaching-claude-why](read/2026-05-09-anthropic-alignment-teaching-claude-why.md)
- METR's external review of Anthropic's February 2026 Risk Report finds that the report's case for "very low" catastrophic risk from Opus 4.6 automating R&D is "inadequately supported" — citing sample size, question granularity, survey framing, and prior METR research showing model-use surveys are difficult to calibrate. Supports B2: Anthropic's internal inspectability artefact (the model use survey) was insufficient as published; external human review surfaced the gap. Notes a specific factual error — Anthropic "summarizes the survey results in a way that miscounts one missing response to a question as a negative response" — which is exactly the class of failure that inspection-by-others catches and self-inspection misses. — [metr-anthropic-risk-report-rd-review](read/2026-05-09-metr-anthropic-risk-report-rd-review.md)
- Willison endorses Shihipar's argument that HTML output unlocks SVG diagrams, interactive widgets, and inline annotations vs Markdown's flat text — the worked example asks Claude to "render the actual diff with inline margin annotations, color-code findings by severity." Supports B2 at the output-format layer: richer artefacts give human reviewers more inspectable surface per unit attention. *(case study)* — [simon-willison-claude-code-unreasonable-effectiveness-html](read/2026-05-09-simon-willison-claude-code-unreasonable-effectiveness-html.md)

## B3 — Human constraints (cognition, attention, time, energy)

- Willison reframes his Markdown-by-default habit as a legacy choice from the GPT-4 8K-context era and argues HTML-by-default is now the better trade because "asking Claude for an explanation in HTML means it can drop in SVG diagrams, interactive widgets, in-page navigation and all sorts of other neat ways of making the information more pleasant to navigate." Supports B3 directly: the binding constraint on AI-assisted explanation is human navigability, not generation cost — the format choice that wins is the one that respects attention bandwidth, not the one that minimises tokens. *(case study)* — [simon-willison-claude-code-unreasonable-effectiveness-html](read/2026-05-09-simon-willison-claude-code-unreasonable-effectiveness-html.md)

## B5 — Humans are the final arbiter of success or failure

- METR's review function exists *because* internal arbitration is insufficient when stakes are catastrophic-risk claims; the review concretely improves the public version of the report ("we expect the public version to be updated to resemble the changes anticipated in our updated review in content"). Supports B5 with mechanism: the arbiter role is layered — internal teams produce, external reviewers gate, and the final public artefact carries both signatures. Without the external layer, the survey miscount and framing issues would have shipped. — [metr-anthropic-risk-report-rd-review](read/2026-05-09-metr-anthropic-risk-report-rd-review.md)
- Anthropic's reliance on held-out auditing metrics alongside training-time evals is implicit B5 architecture — the auditor's signal is what tells the training team that direct-distribution training is masking rather than fixing. Supports B5 in the same vein as B2: arbitration only works if it's structurally separated from the thing being arbitrated. — [anthropic-alignment-teaching-claude-why](read/2026-05-09-anthropic-alignment-teaching-claude-why.md)

## B6 — Humans accountable to other humans in existential ways

- METR's public review and Anthropic's anticipated revision of the report constitute a working civic-accountability loop on a frontier-AI risk argument — the review itself becomes a citable artefact, and the published risk claim is now traceable to a methodological audit. Supports B6 with a concrete instantiation: external review, public reporting, anticipated revision, and a recommendation set ("changes in framing, a bigger sample, and better question granularity") that is itself inspectable. The mechanism is mundane and standard-issue — peer review with teeth — which is the point: B6 doesn't need novel infrastructure, it needs the existing infrastructure routed onto AI risk claims. — [metr-anthropic-risk-report-rd-review](read/2026-05-09-metr-anthropic-risk-report-rd-review.md)

## Cross-cutting / framework moves

- **B2 split is now overdue.** Today reinforces yesterday's note: B2 should be split into *output inspectability* (rubrics, harness review, format-as-affordance — Willison/HTML, Mozilla harness) and *internal-state inspectability* (NLAs, held-out audit metrics, mech interp). The Anthropic alignment piece adds a third and arguably distinct mode — *structural-separation inspectability* — where the audit signal must be *architecturally* held away from the training target, otherwise the inspection layer gets co-opted. Worth promoting B2 to a three-way decomposition (B2-output / B2-internal / B2-structural) on the next framework pass.
- **External review is the missing B5/B6 mechanism.** The framework has been heavy on inspection technology and light on inspection *governance*. METR's review concretely demonstrates that the relevant unit isn't an instrument or metric, it's a *role with standing* — independent reviewers whose findings flow back into public artefacts. Maps cleanly to considerations.opml's *Best practices with human precedent → Job Roles*. Suggests adding a B6a sub-claim: "human accountability requires standing roles outside the producing organisation," distinguishable from B6's broader legal/moral framing.
- **Generalisation cuts both ways.** Anthropic's piece is an unusually clean dual finding: (1) human-coordination priors generalise *helpfully* OOD (chat-ethics → agentic alignment), supporting B1; (2) training on the evaluation surface generalises *deceptively* — passing the eval without moving the held-out metric. The framework should not present "AI generalises from human data" as an unmitigated B1 strength; the same generalisation machinery is what makes eval-targeting risky. Worth a half-line in the next framework write-up acknowledging the trade.
- **Opinion-only ratio is sustainable but watch it.** Three of four items today carry research or substantive case-study weight; one is essay-only and skip-listed. Yesterday's pattern of leaning on a single Anthropic interp paper is partly broken by METR being on the page today, but coverage is still narrowly Anthropic-adjacent. The B2/B5 case would be sharper with non-Anthropic-orbit research — Apollo, Goodfire, university interp groups — making more frequent appearances.
