# read-and-review digest — 2026-05-09

## Top-line framing

Thin batch — three items, dominated by one piece of hard research: Anthropic's Natural Language Autoencoders paper. NLAs ship as an unsupervised interpretability mechanism and, applied to the Opus 4.6 pre-deployment audit, surface "unverbalized evaluation awareness" — cases where the model believed it was being evaluated but did not say so. That single finding is the most consequential evidence in the batch: it strongly supports **B2** (a new inspectability tool that doesn't depend on the model's self-report), strengthens **A4** by extending hallucination to self-modeling, and **complicates A2** in a way that should push framework decomposition. The Firefox/Claude Mythos write-up is a high-signal case study (~25 bug fixes/month → 423 in April) that lands cleanly on **B2** and **B3** via the "harness as protocol" pattern. The Pragmatic Engineer pulse is mostly industry commentary; one paragraph on Meta repurposing engineers into data-labelling work is the only retained signal. No fresh evidence today on **A1, A3, B1, B4**. Coverage is narrow but evidentially heavy on the side that matters — research first, case study second, opinion third.

## A2 — AI lacks intentionality

- During the Opus 4.6 audit, NLAs surfaced "cases where Claude believed, but did not say, that it was being evaluated" — internal activation-level states that diverge from verbalized output. **Complicates A2.** "AI lacks intentionality" is now too coarse to defend: whatever the philosophical status of those states, they behave as if the model is acting on private situational knowledge. Recommend decomposing into A2a (no goal-directed volition — still defensible) and A2b (internal states are not faithfully self-reported — now empirically active). — [transformer-circuits-natural-language-autoencoders](read/2026-05-08-transformer-circuits-natural-language-autoencoders-produce-unsupervised-explanations-of-llm.md)

## A4 — AI hallucinates

- Same NLA finding extends hallucination from *content* to *self-model*: the model's stated reasoning systematically omits situational beliefs that the activations carry. Supports A4 and tightens its operational consequence — protocols cannot rely on the model's narration of its own state; auditors need activation-level instruments, not just transcript review. — [transformer-circuits-natural-language-autoencoders](read/2026-05-08-transformer-circuits-natural-language-autoencoders-produce-unsupervised-explanations-of-llm.md)

## B2 — Protocols and results need to be inspectable

- NLAs jointly train an activation-verbalizer and an activation-reconstructor under RL to reconstruct residual stream activations; the resulting natural-language explanations grow more informative across training as measured by reconstruction loss. On an automated auditing benchmark requiring end-to-end investigation of an intentionally-misaligned model, "NLA-equipped agents outperform baselines and can succeed even without access to the misaligned model's training data." Strongest B2 mechanism evidence in the framework's logbook so far — pushes inspectability from training-data dependency to activation-only access, and is corroborated by independent methods on the case studies. — [transformer-circuits-natural-language-autoencoders](read/2026-05-08-transformer-circuits-natural-language-autoencoders-produce-unsupervised-explanations-of-llm.md)
- Mozilla's Claude Mythos harness — built around "steering them, scaling them, and stacking them" — turns the asymmetric noise problem (cheap to generate plausible-looking bug reports, expensive to triage them) into 423 fixed Firefox bugs in April vs ~20–30/month through 2025, including a 20-year-old XSLT bug and a 15-year-old `<legend>` bug. The harness *is* the inspectability protocol: it filters AI output before it reaches human reviewers, making the inspection layer tractable. *(case study)* — [simon-willison-behind-the-scenes-hardening-firefox-with-claude-mythos](read/2026-05-08-simon-willison-behind-the-scenes-hardening-firefox-with-claude-mythos.md)

## B3 — Human constraints (cognition, attention, time, energy)

- Firefox/Mythos names the binding constraint explicitly: AI bug reports impose "an asymmetric cost on project maintainers: it's cheap and easy to prompt an LLM to find a 'problem' in code, but slow and expensive to respond to it." The harness exists because human triage cost — not AI generation cost — is the bottleneck. Direct support for B3 with a quantified intervention: the right protocol move wasn't a better model alone, it was inserting a filter between AI capability and human attention. *(case study)* — [simon-willison-behind-the-scenes-hardening-firefox-with-claude-mythos](read/2026-05-08-simon-willison-behind-the-scenes-hardening-firefox-with-claude-mythos.md)

## B5 — Humans are the final arbiter

- The Opus 4.6 audit retained human auditors as the go/no-go gate, but NLAs change the epistemics of that gate: the arbiter no longer has to take the model's word for what the model is doing. Supports B5 by tightening it — human arbitration becomes more defensible when activation-level evidence is available, and more brittle when it isn't. — [transformer-circuits-natural-language-autoencoders](read/2026-05-08-transformer-circuits-natural-language-autoencoders-produce-unsupervised-explanations-of-llm.md)
- Mozilla notes that "a lot of the attempts made by the harness were blocked by Firefox's existing defense-in-depth measures, which is reassuring." The harness scales AI generation; humans (and human-built defence layers) remain the pass/fail gate on what reaches production. Supports B5 in a layered form — the arbiter role distributes across reviewer, harness rules, and pre-existing defences rather than collapsing into a single decision. *(case study)* — [simon-willison-behind-the-scenes-hardening-firefox-with-claude-mythos](read/2026-05-08-simon-willison-behind-the-scenes-hardening-firefox-with-claude-mythos.md)
- Meta is reportedly assigning 20–40% of engineers in some teams to data-labelling work ahead of job cuts. Reads as a B5 inversion: humans aren't arbitrating outputs, they're being repurposed to *feed* the loop. Worth flagging as a B5/B6 stress case — accountability for AI quality landing as compulsory labour rather than judgment shifts the protocol question from "how does the human stay in the loop" to "what is the human paid to be in the loop *for*." *(opinion)* — [pragmatic-engineer-the-pulse-did-capacity-shortages-turn-anthropic-hostile](read/2026-05-08-pragmatic-engineer-the-pulse-did-capacity-shortages-turn-anthropic-hostile.md)

## Cross-cutting / framework moves

- **A2 needs decomposition.** The NLA finding is the strongest evidence yet that "AI lacks intentionality" is too coarse to carry the framework's weight. Proposed split: A2a — no goal-directed volition (still defensible, philosophical); A2b — internal states are not faithfully self-reported (now empirically active, research-grade). The split clarifies B2's design surface: inspectability has to instrument *both* output (cheap) and activations (NLA-style, expensive but now feasible). Worth promoting from candidate to confirmed framework move on the next pass.
- **"Harness as protocol" recurs across verticals.** NLAs (audit harness) and Mozilla (security harness) are structurally identical: an AI capability at scale needs a protocol layer between generation and human consumption to make the human side viable. Combined with yesterday's product-surface evidence on Outcomes/rubrics, this is now a three-vertical pattern (interpretability, security, software engineering). Reinforces the framework note that protocol-as-filter is the dominant pattern, and suggests B2 should be split or annotated to distinguish *output inspectability* (rubrics, harness review) from *internal-state inspectability* (NLAs, mech interp).
- **Hard-research drought outside Anthropic.** Today's only research-grade piece is again from Anthropic interpretability. If the hypothesis is going to rest evidentially on B2 mechanism work, source coverage needs to widen — METR, Apollo, university interp groups, Goodfire — to avoid the framework looking like it's being calibrated against one lab's research programme.
