# Project: Long Now Lab 001.1 — Book of Time

The application target for the Prufrock Protocol pitch.

## What it is

The first Lab in Long Now Foundation's *Protocols for the Long Now* series (Lab Series 001), in association with the Protocols Institute. The brief: submit a concept for a new way of marking, experiencing, or making sense of time.

- Top 25 concepts → published in a Long Now print + digital anthology, deposited in the permanent Long Now Library and Archive.
- Top 3 concepts → follow-on investment and development into a working prototype.

## Application form fields

- **Your name** (required)
- **Your email address** (required)
- **Your concept** (required, ≤ 500 words) — explains how the way of thinking about / experimenting with time re-orients imagination, behavior, or decision-making
- **Supporting media** — up to 10 files, max 10 GB each (visual designs, sketches, audio/video ≤ 5 min, etc.)
- **Previous work** — list of relevant prior work with links
- **LinkedIn profile** (optional)
- **Personal website** (optional)
- **Affiliations and community** — channels excited to share work with
- **How did you hear about this Lab?** — radio buttons including "The Protocol Institute"

## Review panel

Interdisciplinary experts in long-term thinking, art, and technology. Reviewed by leading artists, philosophers, and technologists.

## Inspirations cited in the brief

- 10,000-year clocks
- Jonathon Keats's *Centuries of the Bristlecone*
- Long Now Zero (02026 dating)
- Katie Paterson's Future Library (100-year writing capsule)
- "Dog years" concept
- Cosmic Calendar (compresses billions of years into one year)
- Blockchains as "sovereign time" independent of third-party interference
- Doomsday Clock as existential-risk metric

The Prufrock Protocol explicitly engages four of these: blockchain (repurposed for human-authorship authentication rather than financial sovereignty), Future Library (continuous sampling rather than a sealed capsule), Pace Layers (operating across operational/per-line/annual/decennial layers), and Long Now Zero (the codex date convention).
