# Situating Prufrock against existing Long Now work

Poetry is itself among the oldest long nows we have: a four-thousand-year-old form that binds the most concrete of moments to themes that persist across centuries. The Prufrock Protocol takes this intrinsic long-now structure and makes it the timepiece itself. It sits differently from Long Now's existing time-marking work, which largely operates either at monumental scale (the 10,000-year Clock, Centuries of the Bristlecone) or as sealed time-capsule (Katie Paterson's Future Library, opened to readers a century after the writing). Other Long Now work compresses deep time into a single visualisation (the Cosmic Calendar, the Doomsday Clock) or repurposes cryptographic infrastructure to provide "sovereign time" independent of third-party interference. Prufrock is not built for duration alone; it is built for verifiable human authorship across the digital dark age that generative AI now threatens to inaugurate. Where Future Library is a capsule, Prufrock is a continuous sampling protocol: small, random, distributed, ongoing, plural. Where the Clock is monumental and singular, Prufrock is conversational and recursive — each signed contribution depends on a prior one, and time is marked not by waiting but by attending. It uses the same cryptographic primitive Long Now identifies in blockchain — signed, timestamped, third-party-independent records — but redirects it from sovereignty over time to authenticity of human expression across time. It also crosses pace layers in an unusual way: contributions happen at the speed of attention — a moment, a day — while the resulting corpus accretes into something culturally durable. Prufrock's claim is that, in an era when generative AI compresses human concerns toward the statistical mean — averaging into anonymous probability-weighted text — the most valuable thing we can record is the opposite move: an expansion of what is compressed in humanity in aggregate into specific, signed, dated, located instances of individual consciousness, giving specificity to the timeless. The only durable way to record such an expansion is in chains, person by person, line by line, across the world. Poetry has always done this work; the protocol asks whether we can now do it together.
