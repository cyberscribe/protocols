# Long Now Board: Grounded Analysis for the Prufrock Pitch

Drafted 2026-05-10. Replaces the earlier speculative pass with an evidence-grounded reading of each board member's stated positions, public work, and Long Now footprint. Citations inline; "limited public evidence" flagged honestly. Per the project's `to-review/` convention, parked here for Robert's human review — no auto-edits after this drop.

Roster source: [Long Now Board](https://longnow.org/people/board/).

---

## Stewart Brand — Cofounder

Brand's published Long Now essays converge on one durable demand: any new long-term mechanism must be situated inside the [Pace Layers framework](https://longnow.org/ideas/pace-layers/) and must answer his standing question about [Elements of a Durable Civilization](https://longnow.org/ideas/elements-of-a-durable-civilization/) and [The Essential Art of Civilization](https://longnow.org/ideas/the-essential-art-of-civilization/) — what does it preserve, at what layer, and how does it tolerate the friction between layers? He's the author of [*The Clock of the Long Now*](http://sb.longnow.org/) and [*How Buildings Learn*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_Buildings_Learn), so he reads new proposals through "shearing layers" and graceful failure: where does Prufrock break, and does the structure absorb the break or amplify it? His [Pace Layers Thinking talk with Paul Saffo](https://longnow.org/talks/02015-brand-saffo/) is the clearest articulation of the lens he'll bring. He'll also test the proposal against the precedent stack — monastic offices, the Talmud, Rosetta — and dock points for any claim of novelty that ignores those forebears.

## Brian Eno — Cofounder

Eno coined "the long now" in his 01996 [Big Here and Long Now](https://longnow.org/ideas/the-big-here-and-long-now/) essay; he's the source of the ambient/generative aesthetic that sits underneath every successful Long Now artefact (Clock chimes, Janus charts). His installations and software — [*77 Million Paintings*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/77_Million_Paintings), [*Bloom*](http://www.generativemusic.com/) with Peter Chilvers, [*Oblique Strategies*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblique_Strategies) — show the working theory: rule-systems that generate non-repeating output, recombinant by design. The Prufrock authorship matrix (random combinatrix, per the project's own design memory) is squarely in this lineage and worth foregrounding in language he'd recognise. He'll want texture, score, tempo — what does the protocol *sound* and *feel* like? — and will resist any policy-document register. His [The Long Now talk (02003)](https://longnow.org/talks/02003-eno/) and [Long Now, now with Danny Hillis (02014)](https://longnow.org/talks/02014-eno-hillis/) are the best primers on the cadence he expects.

## Mick Costigan — Chair of the Board

Costigan's [bio](https://longnow.org/people/mick-costigan/) frames Long Now's near-term horizon as "Long Q2 — a pivotal quarter-century for our civilization and our foundation," which is the directest evidence of his governance lens: 25-year strategy, AI/sustainability/work as primary thematic axes (his Salesforce Futures remit), and decision-making informed by a decade in scenario planning at GBN/Monitor and a year on the World Economic Forum AI Policy team. As Chair, he carries fiduciary and reputational weight; he'll want clear decision rights between Long Now, the Protocols Institute, and Robert, plus a credible answer to "why Long Now and not a publisher, university, or standalone foundation?" His foresight-team background means he'll respond well to scenario robustness (does Prufrock still work in three plausible futures?) and badly to single-narrative framing. Limited public evidence on his specific aesthetic preferences — pitch governance and scenario rigour to him, not texture.

## Danica Remy — Secretary of the Board

Remy's record is field-building over decades: President of [B612 Foundation](https://b612foundation.org/) (planetary defence research and systems design), co-founder of [Asteroid Day](https://www.asteroidday.org/) (UN-sanctioned 02016, partners with the Government of Luxembourg and ESA), and President of Point Foundation (Whole Earth Catalog's publisher). She co-founded [Global Business Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Business_Network) with Brand, ran ops at Tides, and was VP at The WELL — she has built and operated the institutions Long Now's culture descends from. She'll evaluate Prufrock as a movement, not an artefact: who teaches it, who practises it across decades, how does global awareness scale, what's the public-engagement plan? Asteroid Day is the model — pick a date, build coalitions with governments and partners, generate annual surface area for the idea. Make the proposal answer "who carries this in 2056?" and she's engaged.

## Joe Speicher — Treasurer of the Board

Speicher is Autodesk's first Chief Sustainability Officer and President of the [Autodesk Foundation](https://www.autodesk.org/), with a Columbia development-economics master's and prior operational leadership at [Living Goods](https://livinggoods.org/), the World Bank, and Google.org ([bio](https://longnow.org/people/joe-speicher/)). The throughline is sustainability budgets, ESG strategy, and impact-driven philanthropy that has to defend itself to a corporate board — he's on Autodesk's ESG Steering Committee. As Treasurer, he'll want budget legibility: tiered ask, unit economics, clear delineation between one-time seed and ongoing operating commitment, and what success looks like at five and twenty-five-year horizons. His Living Goods background makes him sympathetic to "small operating team, durable outcomes" patterns over big-build-then-decay grants. Show him the steady-state cost picture and a defensible theory of who funds maintenance after Long Now's seed.

## Patrick Collison — Member of the Board

Collison's [Long Now bio](https://longnow.org/people/patrick-collison/) reads thin but his public footprint is dense: Stripe CEO, co-founder of [Fast Grants](https://fastgrants.org/) (rapid Covid research grant-making), co-author of "Progress Studies" in [The Atlantic (02019)](https://www.theatlantic.com/science/archive/2019/07/we-need-new-science-progress/594946/) with Tyler Cowen, and co-founder of [Arc Institute](https://arcinstitute.org/) (long-horizon biomedical research). The throughline: outlier ambition, intolerance for slow institutional throat-clearing, leverage-per-dollar, and a meta-question about why high-impact work is so rare. He'll read Prufrock for theory of change, why now, why this team, and what it unlocks that nothing else does. He'll forgive an unusual frame (the Eliot literary reference) if it earns its keep — but a small, low-conviction ask reads worse to him than an ambitious one with a credible plan. Do not pad. He reads carefully and quickly.

## David Eagleman — Member of the Board

Eagleman is the closest match in the room to Prufrock's substantive concerns. He's a Stanford neuroscientist whose research programme is built on time perception ([bio](https://longnow.org/people/david-eagleman/), [*The Brain* PBS series](https://www.pbs.org/show/brain-david-eagleman/), [*Livewired*](https://eagleman.com/books/livewired/)), and his book [*Sum: Forty Tales from the Afterlives*](https://eagleman.com/books/sum/) is forty short fictions about death, time, and what persists — Eliot territory. He performed *Sum* as a [musical reading at the Sydney Opera House with Brian Eno](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=eagleman+eno+sum+sydney) and partnered with [Max Richter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Richter) on a *Sum* opera at the Royal Opera House in 02012. His [Long Now talks](https://longnow.org/people/david-eagleman/) include "Six Easy Steps to Avert the Collapse of Civilization" (02010) and "The Brain and The Now" (02016). He'll want any cognitive-or-perceptual claims grounded in evidence or honestly framed as artistic; offer him an empirical research handle (does the protocol measurably alter felt time, attention, or memory?) and he becomes an active collaborator rather than a polite voter.

## Kevin Kelly — Member of the Board

Kelly is Long Now's most prolific archivist and pattern-namer; his Long Now essays are the closest thing to a public taxonomy of long-now mechanisms. Read in sequence — [Generational Theater](https://longnow.org/ideas/generational-theater/), [Movage](https://longnow.org/ideas/movage/), [The Library of Utility](https://longnow.org/ideas/the-library-of-utility/), [Very Long-Term Backup](https://longnow.org/ideas/very-long-term-backup/), [1,000 Years of Forgetting](https://longnow.org/ideas/1000-years-of-forgetting/), [The Need for Long-term Research](https://longnow.org/ideas/the-need-for-long-term-research/) — they map the design space Prufrock has to find a unique cell within. His books [*Out of Control*](https://www.kk.org/outofcontrol/) and [*What Technology Wants*](https://kk.org/books/what-technology-wants/) make him pro-distribution, pro-mutation, pro-remix; he co-founded the [Hackers' Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hackers_Conference) and [WELL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_WELL), and runs [Cool Tools](https://kk.org/cooltools/) and [Recomendo](https://www.recomendo.com/). He'll ask where Prufrock sits in the existing taxonomy (Clock, Rosetta, Bristlecone, Pace Layers, Interval) and how it's designed to be copied and mutated by people Long Now doesn't yet know.

## David Rumsey — Member of the Board

Rumsey's standards are archival: he built a [private collection of 150,000 historical maps](https://www.davidrumsey.com/), made 22,000+ free to the public online (Webby for Technical Achievement, 02002), and donated the collection to Stanford starting 02009 ([bio](https://longnow.org/people/david-rumsey/)). He sits on the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/) board, the John Carter Brown Library, the American Antiquarian Society, the Stanford Library Advisory Board, and the Samuel H. Kress Foundation. His [Mapping Time talk (02004)](https://longnow.org/talks/02004-rumsey/) is on theme. He'll examine Prufrock for artefactual longevity: what physical objects does it produce, what's their provenance and citation chain, what are the digital components' analogue fallbacks, and would a serious institution two centuries hence treat the artefact as evidence rather than ephemera? Format-stability and accession-readiness matter more to him than aesthetic novelty; show him the long tail of the artefact, not just the launch.

## Convergent Themes

Five clusters land for multiple members from independent evidentiary bases:

**Pace-layer placement.** Brand owns this lens directly; Kelly's taxonomy essays operate inside it; Costigan's Long Q2 framing is a temporal-layer claim. Where does Prufrock sit, and what does it do *to* the layer it operates on? Answering this once satisfies three readers.

**Longevity mechanism.** Brand (graceful failure), Rumsey (archival physicality), Kelly (Movage, Library of Utility, Long-Term Backup), and Remy (Asteroid Day's annual cadence) all want a credible answer for how the artefact survives institutional decay. Each comes at it from a different evidentiary base — books, archives, taxonomies, field organising — so the answer needs more than one register.

**Aesthetic-and-experiential texture.** Eno needs the protocol to be felt; Eagleman wants the felt experience either grounded empirically or honestly labelled as art; Brand has spent decades insisting that durability and beauty are not opposites (see [We Are As Gods](https://longnow.org/ideas/stewart-brand-and-brian-eno-on-we-are-as-gods/), with Eno).

**Governance and brand fit.** Costigan (Chair, scenario rigour), Speicher (Treasurer, budget legibility), and Collison (theory-of-change, leverage) form the fiduciary triangle. They share an underlying question: does Prufrock extend Long Now's brand or dilute it, and is the operating commitment proportional to the upside? Different vocabularies, same question.

**Field-building over time.** Remy (Asteroid Day, B612, Tides, Point), Kelly (diffusion and remix), Brand (institutional propagation across decades), and arguably Eno (generative systems that mutate through use). The protocol can't be a single artefact; it has to be designed to grow practitioners.

## Structural Tensions

The hardest single tension to hold in one document: **Eno-vs-Costigan**. The proposal must be aesthetically alive (Eno will not engage with policy-document texture) and governance-legible (Costigan, Speicher, and Collison will not vote on something that reads as artistic vapour). The Prufrock literary frame is asset *or* liability depending on whether it earns its keep — Collison and Eno will both notice if it's decorative. Treat the frame as load-bearing argument or cut it.

The second tension: **Eagleman-vs-Eno on epistemic register**. If Prufrock claims any cognitive or perceptual effect, Eagleman will want it operationalised; Eno's tradition is to leave the effect productively underspecified. The honest move is probably to label the artistic and the empirical claims separately rather than collapse them — Eagleman has spent a career partnering with Eno on exactly that division of labour ([*Sum* at the Sydney Opera House](https://eagleman.com/books/sum/)), so the precedent is in the room.

The third tension: **Collison's "why now"** vs. **Brand's "all real long-term work has precedent."** Both are right. The proposal needs a credible "why this is freshly possible in 02026" *and* a sober survey of forebears. Lead with one and bury the other and you lose half the room.
