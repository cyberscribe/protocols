Amara Osei
Accra, Ghana
The summer I remember had no owner
What does the grave forget that the soil keeps
I named my grief and it answered to nothing
The words we inherit outlive the mouths
Even absence has a temperature in this latitude
She carried the country in her posture
What is given cannot be taken, only forgotten
The body knows the season the mind misremembers
I have been quiet long enough to hear the city
Distance is a kind of keeping — what travels, remains
The poem I meant to write lives in this one instead
Memory is not the past; it is what the past becomes
To outlast the naming is to become the unnamed thing
We were here. The line holds.
Hiroshi Tanaka
Kyoto, Japan
What the pine writes in shadows, snow erases
The water holds the mountain's shape all winter
Every ending is a door that faces east
I write the character for home and recognize nothing
Silence between notes: that is also music
The temple bell rings once for the world's grief
In the garden, the stone does not try to be the stone
What the moon erases, the tide reassembles
I fold the paper and the crane remembers flying
The ink dries and the word becomes a fact
Seven times the blossoms fall and rise
To see the thing fully is to let it go
What I leave in this poem I cannot carry home
The empty bowl still holds the shape of what was fed
Marisol Fuentes
Buenos Aires, AR
I carry what I cannot bury in the common grave
The tango remembers what the dictator forgot
My grandmother's name lives in the disappeared
What survives the burning is not what burns
The river runs between two countries neither owns
I spell the word for mercy in three languages
Every child inherits someone else's war
The pampas remember the horses, not the riders
I wrote the date and the date became a wound
What the body holds, the mind can never quite release
I found my voice in the space between two flags
The disappeared are counted. The count goes on.
To name the wound is not to close it
And still the river moves. And still we name it.
Siobhán Ní Faoláin
Galway, IE
These hands have held what tides forget they moved
The bog preserves what the land cannot say aloud
We named the headland after a woman who drowned
Salt is the oldest word for grief in this language
The stone wall holds without a single nail
What was cleared from this hillside still echoes
The Atlantic does not know it is an edge
I keep the old words in a drawer with my mother's ring
Rain here is a kind of listening
The famine road leads somewhere no map shows
I speak the language of fields to a city that hears nothing
What we buried comes up in the spring like weeds
The last speaker of a language becomes a door
Still: the sea. Still: the stone. Still: this.
Elif Şahin
Istanbul, TR
Between two empires, I misplace my century
The Bosphorus is one word for between
My grandfather prayed in a language the state erased
The minaret holds its silence like a fist
In the bazaar, time is sold by the gram
Two shores, one city: I am the water between
The word for longing in Turkish has no translation
What the conqueror renamed the city still remembers
I am modern in the ruins of three civilisations
Hüzün: the sadness of a place that remembers itself
Every mosque was once something else
I write in the alphabet of an empire I was not born into
The ferry crosses and the city does not choose sides
Between sunset and prayer: a bird. Nothing more.
Meera Pillai
Kochi, IN
The monsoon names each year the same: this one
Every spice is a country that colonised the mouth
The backwaters hold the sky without possessing it
My grandmother's sari is a cartography of grief
Coconut palms bend because the wind keeps asking
We have forty words for the kind of rain this is
The Kathakali mask outlasts the face beneath
I inherit the land and the land inherits the flood
The temple elephant has learned to wait in the dark
What the river gives the sea cannot return
My mother's recipe is a language I am forgetting
The jasmine opens at night because nobody asks it to
Between Sanskrit and Malayalam, I lost my name
The monsoon will return. We are not what returns.

Marcus Webb
Detroit, USA
The pipe runs through the wall and no one sees it
Everything load-bearing hides behind the finish
I know this house from the inside of its walls
The work that holds is never what they photograph
Pressure finds the smallest gap and takes its time
Something leaks for years before anyone notices
I fixed the thing they broke and did not say
The silence after the wrench is its own kind of proof
Heat moves through metal the way grief moves through families
I learned from my father who learned from his hands
What rusts together is what was left untended
The city drains into itself and starts again
Every joint I seal against the dark does hold
Under the floor, the river still runs.
Fatimah Al-Hassan
Nairobi, KE
At 3am the ward is quiet and the clocks keep going
The patient's name is a country I'll never visit
I hold the hand of the one who cannot hold back
We measure life in doses, in vigils, in charts
What leaves the body does not disappear entirely
The night shift ends and the sun has no opinion
I have learned that presence is the only thing
The dying do not ask for explanation, only company
My hands know the weight of loss before my mind does
I wrote the time of death and put my name beside it
What we call miracle is sometimes just more time
The family waits in a room that knows all families
I carry the faces home and set them on the table
Tomorrow the ward fills up and we begin again.
Guo Mei
Chongqing, CN
The machine remembers every shift I work and forget
The quota is a number that has no interest in me
My hands know the weight before the number reads
I sent money home and called it love in a different tongue
The factory bell: my body knows it before my mind
On my lunch break I watched a sparrow stand still
What the river takes from the mountain it gives to the sea
I am far from home in a city I know very well
The dormitory holds forty women. Each is alone.
My daughter will not do this work. I am sure of nothing else.
The conveyor runs. The count increases. I am one of the count.
Today I saw my reflection in the machine and looked away
What is assembled here will be taken apart somewhere else
The shift ends. The river does not end. I end.
