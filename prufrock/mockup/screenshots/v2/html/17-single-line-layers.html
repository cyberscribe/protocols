Layer 01 — Art / Signed line
2026-06-08 · 09:14 GMT · minutes
A single act of attention. Amara Osei opened the app in Accra at dawn. The prior two lines arrived — Shakespeare's seed: "Nor shall death brag…" — she wrote one line and signed it. SHA-3: c4a8f1d2…7b3e. Timestamp sealed. This moment will not change.
Layer 02 — Commerce / Poem
Poem 01 · 14 contributions · June 2026 · days
Her line becomes the first of fourteen. Each subsequent poet receives the prior two lines. Her L01 — "The summer I remember had no owner" — passes to the L02 poet as half of their prompt. The poem closes on day 14.
Layer 03 — Infrastructure / Experiment
Sonnet Experiment Alpha · 14 poems · 1 year
Poem 01 is one of fourteen. All 14 poets write one line per poem per day. By year's end, 196 signed lines form a corpus — the first complete run of the Prufrock Protocol. Amara's L01 appears in 14 of those poems.
Layer 04 — Governance / Protocol
Protocol v1.0 · decades
The protocol rules — two-line prompt, one-line response, cryptographic signing, random interruption timing — exist independently of this experiment. They will govern future runs, with different cohorts, different seeds, different centuries. Her line was produced by these rules and testifies to them.
Layer 05 — Culture / Seed text
Shakespeare · Sonnet 18 · 1609 · 417 years
The seed lines she received were written in London in 1609. "Nor shall death brag thou wander'st in his shade." She responded without knowing who would read her response next. Her summer that had no owner echoes the poem's summer that "shall not fade" — across four centuries of English.
Layer 06 — Culture / Concern
Memory · Domestic · millennia
"The summer I remember had no owner" — this engages Memory and the Domestic: a summer not owned by anyone, already lost to collective forgetting. These concerns appear in Gilgamesh (2100 BCE), in Khayyám (c.1120 CE), in Wordsworth (1798). They are not hers alone; they are the recurring human question.
Layer 07 — Nature / Language
Language · human consciousness · geological
Every word she chose has been used by the dead for thousands of years. "Summer." "Remember." "Owner." These are not her words — she is borrowing them from all prior speakers and lending them to all who come after. This is what language is. The protocol makes this inheritance visible.
